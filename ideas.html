Blessing Ideas

Big and small ways to bless someone

You don't need money to bless somebody — most of the best blessings only cost a little of your time and attention. Here are some ideas to get you started. Pick one this week and see where God leads.

Just a few minutes Free

  • Hold the door, look the person in the eye, and smile.
  • Let someone with only a few items go ahead of you in line.
  • Return your shopping cart — and someone else's while you're at it.
  • Learn the cashier's name from their tag and thank them by name.
  • Text someone and tell them one specific thing you appreciate about them.
  • Give a sincere compliment to a stranger.
  • Tell a manager when an employee did a great job.
  • Leave an encouraging note or a favorite Bible verse where someone will find it.

Give your time Free

  • Carry groceries for someone who's struggling with them.
  • Sit with someone eating alone and just listen.
  • Visit a nursing home and chat with residents who don't get many visitors.
  • Mow a lawn, rake leaves, or pull weeds for someone who can't.
  • Offer to watch the kids so a tired parent can rest.
  • Give someone a ride to a doctor's appointment, the store, or church.
  • Help someone load or unload a moving truck.
  • Teach someone a skill you have — cooking, fixing, sewing, reading.

Around the neighborhood Free

  • Bring a neighbor's trash cans up from the curb.
  • Pick up litter along the street or at a park.
  • Introduce yourself to a new neighbor and welcome them.
  • Check on older neighbors during storms or hot weather.
  • Write a thank-you note to your mail carrier, a teacher, or the local fire station.
  • Sweep or shovel a neighbor's walk along with your own.

For someone who's hurting Free

  • Ask, “Can I pray for you?” — and then pray with them right there.
  • Send a handwritten card to someone grieving, especially months later when the cards stop coming.
  • Remember a hard anniversary and reach out that day.
  • Sit with a friend in a hospital waiting room.
  • Listen without trying to fix anything.

When you can give a little more Small cost

  • Buy the coffee for the person behind you.
  • Pay for someone's meal and slip out quietly.
  • Tuck a gas card into a note for someone on the road.
  • Bring a meal to a new mom, someone who's sick, or a family going through a hard time.
  • Tip generously and leave a kind note with it.
  • Carry warm socks or bottled water in the car to hand out.

Big blessings Plan ahead

  • Organize a yard-work day for a widow or someone who is homebound.
  • Gather friends or your church to collect items for a shelter or pregnancy center.
  • Host a free car wash or a community meal.
  • Help with a home repair for someone who can't afford it.
  • Mentor a young person — show up week after week.
